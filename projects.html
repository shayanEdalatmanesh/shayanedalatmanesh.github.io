Machine learning & data

League of Legends match-outcome predictor

Logistic regression in PyTorch on 2025 ranked-match data, with a full train / tune / evaluate workflow.

Question
Can a player’s win or loss be classified from end-of-match statistics?
Data
The League of Legends Match Dataset 2025 (Kaggle, CC BY 4.0): 40,410 player records, divided almost evenly between wins and losses. Split 80/20 for training and testing. Features were standardized using the training set only.
Approach
Logistic regression written as a PyTorch module (BCE loss, SGD, 1,000 full-batch epochs). Compared plain training with L2 regularisation and a learning-rate sweep, evaluated with a confusion matrix, classification report and ROC curve, and inspected the learned weights.
Result
About 75% test accuracy (75.0% plain, 74% with L2), and a ROC AUC of 0.82. The weights are interpretable: deaths is the strongest negative factor, assists and kills the strongest positive ones.

Non-Hermitian SSH chain

Exploring the topological properties of a one-dimensional chain, partially connected to a surface.

What
The SSH model is the go-to model for understanding one-dimensional topological systems.
Question
How does substrate coupling changes the spectrum and edge states of the SSH model?
Result
It enables controlled manipulation of zero-energy boundary modes, revealing their evolution and enhancement in relation to exceptional points through tunable coupling and selective site decoupling.
